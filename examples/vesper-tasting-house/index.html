I — The Doctrine

We cook as if the ingredient were watching. Nothing arrives on the plate that cannot name its field, its tide or its farmer. Restraint is our loudest flavour, and every course is built to be remembered in the body long after it has left the tongue.

The Monograph

Four chapters of the house, each turned like a page: the geometry of the plate, the vault beneath the floor, the pavilions and the brigade.

PLATE I — GEOMETRY
Chapter One

The geometry of appetite

Every composition is drafted on paper before it touches porcelain: a golden spiral of sauce, a negative space where the eye rests, a single herb set by tweezer at the last breath before service.

Plateware
Hand-thrown by a local ceramicist
Tools
Offset tweezers, copper spoons, a ruler
PLATE II — THE VAULT
Chapter Two

Thirty feet below service

The subterranean vault holds a living library: grower Champagne, forgotten Jura, sake aged in cedar and our own vinegars resting in barrel. Guests of the Reserve pairing descend between movements nine and ten.

Bottles
3,400, catalogued by soil
Temperature
12.5°C, unbroken since opening
PLATE III — THE PAVILION
Chapter Three

Rooms that keep secrets

Three private pavilions, each with its own pass to the kitchen and a menu written for the table alone. Lighting is set to the course: amber for fire, near-darkness for the course eaten by touch.

PLATE IV — THE BRIGADE
Chapter Four

Eleven hands, one breath

Led by Chef Name, formerly of Restaurant and Restaurant. The brigade plates in total silence; the only sound at the pass is the bell.
